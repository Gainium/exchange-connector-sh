# 038 — An unfilled Bitget spot limit order is reported at price 0

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 — expected

`BitgetExchange.convertSpotOrder` maps a classic (v2) Bitget spot order to a
`CommonOrder`. For a LIMIT order, `price` is the price the order trades at:
the average fill price once something has filled, and the order's own limit
price while nothing has. An order resting on the book is reported at its
limit price, never at `0`.

### 1.2 — actual

For a LIMIT order `price` is `priceAvg` alone. Bitget sends `priceAvg: "0"`
for an order that has not filled, so a resting limit order — including the
read `spot_openOrder` does right after placing one — comes back with
`price: "0"`.

The bot engine classifies a placed order by comparing the returned price to
the price it asked for: a price that differs means the order traded at
market. `"0"` differs, so every grid / DCA limit order placed on a classic
Bitget spot account is stored as a MARKET order. Everything that looks for
resting LIMIT orders (missed-fill recovery, the dashboard, health checks)
does not see them.

### 1.3 — unchanged

- A LIMIT order with a fill: `price` is `priceAvg`.
- A MARKET order: `basePrice`, else `priceAvg`, as before.
- Every other field, and the futures and UTA converters (both already fall
  back to the order's `price`).

## 2. Evidence

- Resting (`NEW`) orders a grid bot placed on a classic Bitget spot account
  are stored with `type: MARKET`, `executedQty: 0` and `price` equal to the
  requested price. The bot's log shows them saved by the placement-response
  path (`Save order <id>`), the only path that assigns `type`.
- In that path a requested-LIMIT order is classified MARKET only when the
  returned `price` is truthy and numerically different from the requested
  price (no fills are ever returned, `executedQty` is 0). The stored `price`
  equal to the requested one means the returned price was exactly `"0"`
  (the path then falls back to the requested price). That is `priceAvg`.
- The UTA converter's own fixtures model an unfilled order with
  `avgPrice: '0'`; the classic futures converter already handles a zero
  `priceAvg` with `+priceAvg || +price`.

## 3. Assumption

Bitget's v2 spot order record carries `priceAvg: "0"` until the order fills,
and `price` is the limit price. Hypothesis, supported by the evidence above.
